Branch trace, second pass — making the fork look clickable

The branch line ships and works: click it and you are in the branch. But it does not read as something you can click. It was tuned to weigh less than a message — grey fill, muted grey text, flanked by two hairlines — and that is exactly the costume a date separator wears. The arrow that promises a destination only appears on hover, so at rest the row announces nothing.

Three ways out below, plus the shipped line for comparison. Variant A follows the user's own idea (2026-08-12): put the fork in a bubble. The argument for it is stronger than "bubbles are visible" — /btw and /branch were typed into the composer. They were a user turn. A bubble on the user's side is not decoration, it is the honest record of what happened at that point in the transcript.

What stays fixed from 2026-08-09

  • Anchor = the last message of the parent chat at creation time; the backend decides it.
  • Selection branches get nothing — their coloured passage is the finer trace.
  • The raw command /btw / /branch in the composer's dashed chip, its own icon per command, fold after 2, spinner while opening, themed landing glow.

05 Recommendation and open questions

Recommendation: variant A, the doorway bubble

Not because bubbles are louder, but because the shape finally matches the fact. A /btw or /branch was a turn the user took in this chat — the one thing the grey separator hid. Putting it on the user's side makes the transcript readable as a history of actions, and gives the row a legitimate reason to look like a control.

  • Tint, not fill. blue-50 ground, blue-700 text, inset blue-100 ring, user corner radius. One step lighter than a user message, several steps louder than today.
  • Verb and arrow at rest. "Open branch →" in the second line is the fix for the actual complaint; hover only deepens the tint and nudges the arrow.
  • The typed command stays verbatim in the dashed composer chip, with its own icon per command — both decisions from 2026-08-09 survive unchanged.
  • Message count as a quiet meta on the right of the action row: it answers "is there anything in there?" without a second click. Drops out while loading so nothing reflows.
  • Fold after two, "show more" as a plain accent link rather than a grey pill.
If A feels too heavy in the running app, variant C is the fallback and costs almost nothing: the same BranchTrace.tsx, accent tint at rest, verb and arrow always visible. It fixes the affordance without touching the transcript's rhythm. Variant B is deliberately not recommended — see the three-fork column in §03.

To decide before building

  1. Side. User's side (right), as drawn — or left with the assistant, so the fork never competes with your own questions?
  2. Message count. Worth the extra query per trace entry, or drop it and keep the action row to the verb alone?
  3. Fold. Two bubbles then a link — or one bubble, given a bubble weighs more than a line?
  4. Selection branches. Still no trace at all (2026-08-09), or does the doorway bubble change that answer now that the trace no longer looks like noise?
  5. The rules. A drops the two hairlines entirely. Keep a single faint rule behind the bubble to mark "this happened at this point in time", or is the position enough?
