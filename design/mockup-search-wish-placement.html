Where the search-wish card lives

The card itself is settled (variant C). The open question is placement: today it is a bordered card inside the answer bubble, which reads as a window inside a window. Every bubble below wears its theme's real skin from index.css, so the nesting looks the way it does in the app.

Measured in the running app, 2026-08-25

The card is a child of .prose.select-text — the answer bubble itself. In four of five themes that bubble already has a frame and a hard offset shadow: Mushroom Kingdom 2px #26264f + 4px ink, Ink Blue 1.5px #1c2b4a + 4px, Hyrule 1px + 4px, Matrix a 2 px left edge + 4px. Only Simply Blue has none.

On top of that, Mushroom Kingdom repaints every border-gray-* as the same ink, so a nested card cannot be quieter than its container — both frames are the same colour and weight. That is the whole effect: not one card too many, but two identical frames.

Precedent in the codebase: the comment at index.css:749 records the same bug from 2026-08-11 — the /btw fold-out reused .prose and “the answer sat in a bubble within a bubble”. The fix there was to stop the bubble skin from applying, not to restyle the child.

§02 D — footer of the bubble

Same content, no second frame: the card loses its border, surface and shadow and becomes a footer inside the bubble, separated by one hairline rule. One box on screen instead of two.

Gains

Nothing nests, in any theme — the fix works by subtraction, not by tuning colours. Reads as part of the answer, which it is: a caveat about that answer.

Costs

The key field then sits on the bubble's own tinted stock rather than on white, so the input needs its own frame to stay legible. Less separable: a reader skimming may not see where the answer ends.

§03 E — a sibling under the bubble

The card leaves the bubble and sits beneath it as its own element, indented and joined by a short tick. It may keep a frame, because it is no longer a frame inside a frame.

Gains

Keeps the card's own surface, so the key field stays on white and the button keeps its contrast. Clearly a separate object: an aside about the answer, not part of it.

Costs

Two stacked boxes still cost vertical room, and in themes with hard offset shadows two shadows fall in a row. Needs a real change in MessageBubble: the card moves out of the prose element, so anything keyed to that container (bubble skin, selection, highlights) must be re-checked.

§04 F — note in the bubble, form on the composer

The bubble keeps only a one-line note. Pressing it opens the key field above the composer — where typing happens anyway — so no form is ever drawn inside a message.

Gains

Messages stay messages and forms stay at the composer, which is the app's existing division. The note is one line, so repeated answers cost almost nothing.

Costs

The action lands far from the thing that prompted it — the reader clicks at the top and the UI changes at the bottom. Ambiguous with several such answers on screen: which one is the key for? Most new machinery of the four.

§05 G — no surface at all

The note keeps its place in the bubble but drops every box: an accent edge, two lines, the action as a text link. Nothing can nest because there is nothing to nest.

Gains

Lightest of the four and immune to any theme's border rules. Two lines high.

Costs

No button — a text link is the weakest possible ask, and the whole point of the redesign was that the button was unreadable. The key field, when open, still needs some frame.
