Search-wish card — three redesigns

The card that appears under an answer when the model wanted to search the web but had no Tavily key. Source today: MessageBubble.tsx → SearchWishCard, built from mockup-onboarding-flow.html §06. Every colour below is a standard token (blue-*, gray-*), so all five themes hold.

What makes today's card look off

  1. Blue on blue on blue. The card is blue-50/60 and the button is blue-50 — a 4 % lightness gap. The button reads as a hole in the card, not as something to press.
  2. A full-width centred button for an optional aside. It is the largest object in the card, so a side remark looks like the answer's main call to action.
  3. Three greys stacked in three rows (gray-700, gray-500, gray-400) fade downwards — and the faintest row carries the best news, free, 1000 searches a month.
  4. A <br> instead of structure. The consequence and the price line share one paragraph, so nothing groups.
  5. No visible hierarchy for the query. “current weather” is the one concrete fact in the card and it is set in the same 11 px grey as everything else.

§02 Variant A — quiet note

No card at all. An accent edge, one sentence, and the action as a text link on the meta row. The card stops competing with the answer it annotates.

Gains

Lightest of the three; nothing to dismiss because nothing intrudes. The query becomes the visual anchor. Height drops from 5 rows to 2.

Costs

A text link is easy to miss — if adding a key is a goal, this variant works against it. No frame means the note can blur into the answer's own text.

§03 Variant B — one row, details on demand

A single pill-shaped row: what happened on the left, the action on the right. The query and the price move behind a click, because on the second sighting the reader already knows them.

Gains

Smallest footprint and a real button — the two goals that fight in the other variants. Survives repetition: five answers in a row each carry one thin line, not five cards.

Costs

Hides both the query and the benefit — one row has no space to say that the free tier covers a day's work, so the reader must click to learn why a key is worth having. Truncates in a narrow column. Adds a state (open/closed) the app must remember.

§04 Variant C — structured card

Stays a card, but on surface rather than blue: the accent is a 2 px strip and a badge, the action is a filled blue-700 button sized to its label, and the price line sits in a separate footer instead of trailing off the body text.

Superseded in part: the card's own frame and surface were dropped on 2026-08-25 — inside the answer bubble they read as a window inside a window. Content and hierarchy below still hold; for where the card sits, see mockup-search-wish-placement.html (variant D).

Measured in this page, and it constrains this variant most: no accent fill reaches 4.5:1 in all five themes. Against its own white token, blue-600 gives only 2.89:1 in Hyrule and 4.31:1 in Mushroom Kingdom; blue-700 — used above — gives 6.70 / 6.70 / 5.65 / 3.92 / 13.83. Hyrule's turquoise ramp has no pair that clears AA for a 12 px label, so C leans hardest on the element the themes serve worst, and A leans on it least.

Gains

Only variant where the button is unmistakably a button. Footer separates fact from offer, so no grey fade. Room for the three states' longer copy without re-flowing.

Costs

Still the tallest and heaviest of the three — a card under every answer is a lot of furniture. A filled brand button pulls attention away from the answer itself.

§05 The other two states, in each variant

The component has three states, not one: the wish (above), a rejected key, and an exhausted allowance. In the latter two, “add a key” is the wrong ask — the reader would retype the same wrong key, or pay for nothing. Both keep gray-* only: per CLAUDE.md, red-* is reserved for errors and destructive actions, and neither of these is one.

§06 The copy, in three registers

Today's text says what did not happen and leaves the rest to inference. Two facts are missing: a Tavily key is the only way Syflo searches at all (ADR-0012 dropped SearXNG), and the free tier is not a trial but ~33 searches a day. The variants above use register 2; 1 and 3 are the same two facts, dialled down and up.

1 · Plain

Answered from memory. Web search needs a Tavily key — 1000 a month, free.

Aus dem Gedächtnis beantwortet. Websuche braucht einen Tavily-Schlüssel — 1000 im Monat, kostenlos.

The fact, no argument. Says nothing about whether the allowance is enough.

2 · Benefit named  ·  used above

A free Tavily key turns web search on — 1000 a month, ~33 a day.

Ein kostenloser Tavily-Schlüssel schaltet die Websuche frei — 1000 im Monat, ~33 am Tag.

One line, both facts: what the key unlocks, and the allowance in a unit the reader can judge — per day, not per month.

3 · Sales pitch

Switch on live web search, free. 1000 searches a month is a full working month — no card, no bill.

Live-Websuche freischalten, kostenlos. 1000 Suchen im Monat sind ein ganzer Arbeitsmonat — ohne Karte, ohne Rechnung.

Strongest pull, and the only one that reads as marketing inside an answer. “no card, no bill” is a claim about Tavily's terms — it has to stay true.

Numbers used: 1000 ÷ 30 ≈ 33 a day. Worth checking against the real free tier before shipping — if Tavily's monthly figure changes, “about 33 a day” has to change with it, in strings.ts, EN and DE in parallel.
