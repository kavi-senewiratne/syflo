Reference links — clickable citations, two doors, no switch

Every citation in the paper is a link: the numbers in the running text, the author-year forms in brackets, and every row of the reference list at the end. There is no toggle — one permanent, quiet look, no setting to find or explain.

There is also only one gesture: click a citation, get a card. The reference list at the end behaves exactly like a citation in the running text — same underline, same click, same card, same two doors: Browser and Open in Syflo. Nothing new to learn at the bottom of the paper.

02 Two citation styles, one mechanism
Papers cite in two shapes: numeric — [16], [38, 24, 15] — and author-year — (Vaswani et al., 2017), (Bengio & Glorot, 2010; Saxe et al., 2013). Both were found in the stored papers (13 numeric, 2 author-year among the annotated ones). The style makes no difference: hyperref writes one rect per cited work either way, so [38, 24, 15] is three separate click targets and (A, 2010; B, 2013) is two. The commas between them stay dead text.

Measured, not assumed: the raw rects really do include the trailing comma ("38,", ", 3]") and sometimes clip the bracket. The existing word-snap helper in pdf/selection.ts already solves exactly this class of problem for selections — the same trick grows each rect out to its whole citation token.
No <a> elements. An anchor over the text layer would swallow mousedown and break the drag-select that Syflo's branching rests on. The rects are hit-tested inside the existing click handler instead — only for a click that carried no drag and left no selection.
03 The card, and the two doors
Click, not hover — a hover card would fire constantly while the eye scans a dense paragraph. Four lines and two doors, nothing else: title, one meta line, two lines of abstract. No badge repeating the number you just clicked, no footnote explaining what the buttons do. The state lives in the door — a paywalled paper reads No free PDF instead of an explanation underneath.

04 The reference list behaves exactly like a citation
A row of the reference list is the same thing as [24] in the running text — so it gets the same treatment, not a second one. Same dotted underline on the title, same click, same card, same two doors. No buttons appearing on hover, no separate vocabulary at the bottom of the paper. Both list shapes work: the numbered list and the alphabetical author-year list.

05 A reference that already became a tree

06 Papers without annotations — three levels
Not every PDF carries its links. What a paper offers decides what the reader gets, and the difference must be visible in the page itself, never announced in a banner: an underline that is there, or an underline that is not. Nothing to dismiss, nothing to read.

07 What happens after the click
Syflo has no toast system, so nothing is announced in a floating strip. The card the reader is already looking at does the talking — exactly like the FloatingPopup during a branch: the button keeps its place and size, only its icon and label change. When the download finishes, the second door has quietly become Go to tree; when it fails, the card says so and the remaining door takes over.

08 What the probe actually measured
Numbers from page.getAnnotations() over the papers already stored in uploads/papers/ — not estimates.
QuestionResultDetail
Are inline citations machine-readable? Yes, in LaTeX PDFs 15 of 30 papers carry cite.* link annotations — 76 to 228 per paper, the BibTeX key in the destination, the destination pointing at the reference row.
Numeric or author-year? Both, same mechanism 13 numeric ("[20]", "[38,"), 2 author-year ("(Vaswani et al., 2017)", "(Bengio & Glorot, 2010"). hyperref emits one rect per cited work in both styles.
Is [38, 24, 15] one target or three? Three Rects at x=164, 179, 194, each ~12 pt wide, each with its own destination. A click is never ambiguous in an annotated PDF.
Are the rects clean? No — snap needed They include the trailing comma ("38,") and sometimes clip a bracket. The existing word-snap helper handles exactly this.
What about unannotated PDFs? Mostly level 2 Of 30 papers: 15 annotated (level 1), 9 with a text layer but no annotations (level 2) — and those 9 are all copies of one document, so the corpus is skewed. 6 files had no citations to find; exactly one of them is a true scan with an empty text layer, the rest are not research papers at all.
Can a reference be resolved? Usually OpenAlex referenced_works[]; fetchReferences() already exists in backend/openalex.js:696, unused. ~40 candidates to match against, not the open web.
Can Syflo open it as a PDF? Depends on OA Only with a downloadable open-access PDF — high in CS/arXiv, low for Elsevier & co. Everything else takes Door A.
Does it break text selection? Not if built right No anchor elements over the text layer. Rects hit-tested in the existing click handler, only for a click with no drag and no selection.
