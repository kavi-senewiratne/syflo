Citation card — four ways to lay out the same three facts

Everything below shows the identical reference, in the shell already built (CitationCard: FloatingPopup's rounded-2xl, the uppercase overline, the gray-50 footer with stacked blue-50 doors). Only the middle band differs — authors, year, citation count, venue.

Variant A is what is running right now. B, C and D each answer the same complaint from a different angle: the block reads as a list of facts, and the eye has no obvious place to land.

Why B: the authors are the one line a reader actually reads — they decide "do I know this work?" — so they get plain text at body weight, not an icon row. Year, citation count and venue are lookup values; they belong together on one quiet line, and their icons make that line scannable without separators.

The reference list — one rule for the underline
Marking whole entries made the bibliography look struck through; falling back to "underline everything when the title can't be located" made it look random — some rows marked under the authors, some under the title, some end to end (user report 2026-08-09). The fix is a rule, not more coverage.

One rule: an underline always sits on the title. A row whose title cannot be located stays plain — losing a click target is a smaller price than a mark that means three different things.
